Know What You're Comparing

Use these essential criteria to evaluate insurance policies and understand the details that can affect your coverage.

Policy Feature What to Look For Where to Verify
Premium Total premium payable, payment frequency, applicable taxes, and renewal costs. Official quotation and premium schedule
Coverage Amount Sum insured, death benefit, or other applicable insured benefit, depending on the policy type. Policy schedule and benefit illustration
Waiting Periods Initial waiting periods, specified-condition waiting periods, and pre-existing disease conditions where applicable. Policy wording and Customer Information Sheet
Exclusions Events, conditions, treatments, or circumstances that are excluded or subject to restrictions. Exclusions and special conditions
Additional Costs Applicable co-payments, deductibles, room-rent limits, sub-limits, and other expenses payable by you. Benefit schedule and policy terms
Hospital Network Eligible hospitals, cashless treatment availability, and the applicable claims process. Insurer's current hospital network
Renewal Terms Renewal conditions, policy duration, payment deadlines, and applicable continuity benefits. Policy wording and renewal terms
Claims and Support Claim submission steps, required documents, support channels, and grievance procedures. Official claims guide and support details

These are comparison criteria, not guaranteed benefits. Coverage, eligibility, exclusions, and costs vary by policy. Life insurance and critical illness policies may have different benefit structures from health insurance. Always review the applicable policy documents before purchasing.

Understand Your Options Before You Decide

Insurance terminology can feel complicated. Start with these common questions to understand the details that matter.

Explore Your Next Step
How do I choose the right insurance plan?

Start by identifying your protection needs, budget, financial responsibilities, and eligibility. Compare coverage, exclusions, waiting periods, additional costs, and claim conditions before selecting a policy.

Should I choose a policy based only on its premium?

No. A lower premium does not necessarily mean better value. Review the coverage provided, applicable limits, exclusions, deductibles, co-payments, and other conditions to understand what you receive for the price.

What is a waiting period in health insurance?

A waiting period is a specified period during which certain benefits or conditions may not yet be covered. The duration and applicability depend on the policy terms, so check the relevant waiting-period clauses before purchasing.

What should I check in policy exclusions?

Review excluded treatments, illnesses, events, circumstances, and any restrictions that may affect a claim. Also check disclosure requirements and special conditions stated in the policy documents.

Can I have more than one insurance policy?

Depending on the product and applicable rules, you may hold multiple policies. How benefits are paid differs by policy type and claim circumstances. Review coordination rules, disclosure requirements, and the terms of each policy.

Which documents should I review before buying?

Review the policy wording, policy schedule, Customer Information Sheet where applicable, premium quotation, benefit illustration for life insurance where applicable, and any additional terms supplied by the insurer.

Answers are general information, not a guarantee of coverage. The actual benefits, exclusions, eligibility, and claim outcomes depend on the applicable policy terms.
